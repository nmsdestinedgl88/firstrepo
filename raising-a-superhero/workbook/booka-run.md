## 4. Concept (Phase 4) and the user's redirect

Working title: **Raising a Superhero**. Subtitle: *A Parent's Activity Workbook for Turning ADHD Traits into Strengths and Growing Your Patience*.

The report proposed a dual-track book (parent track plus child activities). The user redirected to a **purely parent-facing** book with simple activities to do together. This is a better fit for the evidence: it removes the child reading load and the "wrong voice" complaint entirely, and puts the parent patience angle at the centre.

Differentiators carried into the build: one voice (the parent's), short activities, a Guardian Pause patience practice in every mission, real human-edit standard, and a non-medical stance stated on the copyright page.

## 5. Solve analysis (Phases 5 and 6)

**First pass, shortfalls found:**
1. Activities could drift into generic parenting advice. Fix: each mission has a fixed anatomy (Idea, Steps, Say This, Notice, Guardian Pause, If It Isn't Working) so it must be doable in the room.
2. Patience could be a single chapter. Fix: a Guardian Pause in every mission, a Patience Check-In opener on every module, plus a six-page Patience Toolkit.
3. Risk of "repetitive" reviews. Fix: shared framing is stated once (Framework page); module openers do not restate it; scripts are short and specific.
4. Medical-claim risk. Fix: no outcome promises, no statistics, no diet or medication content, notice on page 2, support page at the back.
5. Superhero language could echo comic IP. Fix: no real character names or the term "kryptonite"; framework uses *Power, Pressure Point, Sidekick Tool, Mission, Guardian Pause, Debrief*.

**Second pass (optimised concept):** the fixes above are all implemented in the draft. Residual weakness: the book cannot be tested with real parents before launch, and the activity ideas are practical but not clinically validated. That is acceptable for a non-medical workbook, provided no efficacy claims are made. **Result: passes, with that stated limit.**

## 8. Voice and quality standards (so every page stays on tone)

1. **Warm, calm, plain.** Speak to a tired parent as a respected peer. Reading level about grade 6 to 7. Short sentences. No jargon.
2. **Never shaming.** No "should", "must" or "failing". The parent is the Hero Guardian, the child is a hero in training. Say "child with ADHD", not "ADHD child".
3. **Honest limits.** Use "many", "often", "can". No statistics, no outcome promises, no diet, supplement or medication content. Professionals are named as the place for those decisions.
4. **No repetition.** Framing is stated once. Each mission has one idea.
5. **Doable tonight.** Household items, 10 to 20 minutes, a clear first step. Always an "If It Isn't Working" fallback.
6. **Patience is a skill, practised.** A one-minute Guardian Pause in every mission, not a lecture.
7. **Reading load stays low.** Child never reads. Paragraphs of 2 to 4 sentences. Scripts are one line.
8. **Human edit before release.** Read aloud, cut anything generic, check each activity can be done by a real family.
