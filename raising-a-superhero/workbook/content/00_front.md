## WELCOME | Welcome, Hero Guardian
If you are holding this book, you are probably tired.

Not the kind of tired a good night's sleep fixes. The kind that comes from saying the same sentence four times, from the morning scramble for shoes, from the phone call from school, from the quiet worry at 10 p.m. about whether you are doing this right.

First, a few things that are true. You are not failing. Your child is not broken. And the fact that you opened a book about this tells me you are exactly the kind of parent your child needs.

This workbook is built on one simple idea. The traits that make daily life hard for many kids with ADHD often have a stronger side. Intense focus can become deep skill. Endless energy can become drive. Fast, jumpy ideas can become creativity. Big feelings can become a big heart. None of this happens by magic, and none of it replaces the support your child may need. It happens in small, ordinary moments, when an adult notices a trait, gives it somewhere useful to go and stays calm enough to see it through.

That last part is the hard part. So this book has two jobs. It gives you simple activities to do with your child, and it gives you small, practical ways to build your own patience while you do them.

There is no grand plan to follow and no homework for your child. Most activities take ten to twenty minutes. You can start anywhere. You can skip a page. You can write in the margins, spill tea on it and come back after a rough week.

You are the Hero Guardian here. Your child is the hero in training. Let's begin.

## HOWTO | How to Use This Book
This book works best in small bites. Here is the rhythm that suits most families.

**One module at a time.** There are eight modules. Each one focuses on a single trait and the strength it can grow into. Plan about one to two weeks per module, though you can go faster or slower.

**One mission at a time.** Each module has five missions. A mission is a short activity you do with your child, usually ten to twenty minutes, with little or no preparation. Read the left page before you start. Fill in the right page afterward, even if it is just a few words.

**One Guardian Pause per mission.** Every mission ends with a tiny patience practice for you. It takes under a minute. These add up faster than you would expect.

**One Debrief per module.** At the end of each module, you look back at what worked and choose what to keep. This is where the real learning lives, so please don't skip it.

**Make it fit.** The ages in mind are roughly five to twelve, but every child is different. If a mission feels too young or too old, change it. If your child says no, try again another day or in another form. Forcing it defeats the point.

**Keep it light.** If an activity turns into a battle, stop. The goal is connection first. A mission that ends in laughter after two minutes has done its job.

**Use the back of the book.** The Patience Toolkit, Phrase Bank, 30-Day Mission Tracker and Wins Log are there to be used between missions. Flip to them whenever you need them.

Words you will see again and again:

- **Power:** the trait, seen from its strong side.
- **Pressure Point:** the place where the trait causes friction.
- **Sidekick Tool:** a simple strategy that helps.
- **Mission:** the activity.
- **Guardian Pause:** your patience move.

## FRAMEWORK | The Hero Framework
Every module in this book follows the same simple shape. Learn it once and the rest of the book gets easier.

TABLE
Step | What It Means | Example
Spot the Power | Notice the trait and name its strong side | Your child loses track of time while building. That is deep focus.
Name the Pressure Point | Be honest about where it causes trouble | Stopping the build for dinner ends in tears.
Choose a Sidekick Tool | Pick one small strategy to try | A ten-minute and five-minute warning before the switch.
Run the Mission | Do a short activity together | Child teaches you what they are building.
Take a Guardian Pause | Practice patience in the moment | Slow breath out before you say the warning.
Debrief | Look back and keep what worked | Warnings helped. Dinner still needs a snack first.
ENDTABLE

Why this shape? Because it keeps you out of two traps. One trap is only seeing the problems. The other is pretending there are none. The Hero Framework asks you to hold both at once: this trait is a gift in one setting and a struggle in another, and your job is to help your child tell the difference.

You do not need to get it right the first time. You just need to try something small, watch what happens and adjust.
