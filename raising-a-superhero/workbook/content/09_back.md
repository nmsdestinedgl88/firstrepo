## PT-1 | Patience Toolkit: The Five-Second Pause
This is the smallest patience tool in the book, and the one you will use most. The Five-Second Pause sits between what happens and what you do about it. Five seconds is not long, but it is often enough to change how a moment goes.

The pause has four short steps. Learn them now, while things are calm, so your body knows them when it counts.

1. **Stop.** Freeze your body and your mouth. Do not say the first thing.
2. **Breathe.** Take one slow breath in and a longer breath out. Let your shoulders drop.
3. **Name it.** Say silently: "I'm frustrated. My child is struggling. Both are true."
4. **Choose.** Ask: "What does my child need right now? What do I want to say?"

When you use the pause, you are not ignoring the problem or letting things slide. You are choosing a response instead of reacting. Some days you will manage it and some days you will not. Both are fine. Every attempt strengthens the habit.

## PT-2 | Patience Toolkit: Guardian Reset Cards
Use these six cards when you feel your patience fraying. Read one aloud, copy it onto a sticky note or photograph this page and keep it on your phone.

CARDS
Breathe Out | Make your out-breath longer than your in-breath. Do it three times.
Drop Your Shoulders | Notice where you are tense. Unclench your jaw and hands.
Lower Your Voice | Speak more softly and more slowly. A quiet voice is easier to hear.
Get Curious | Ask yourself: "What is my child trying to tell me with this behaviour?"
Name the Need | Is it hunger, tiredness, worry or a need for connection? Meet that first.
Step Away Safely | If you need space and your child is safe, say "I need a minute" and take it.
ENDCARDS

## PB-1 | Phrase Bank: Instead of, Try
Words matter, but so does tone. These swaps are starting points. Use your own voice and adapt them to your child.

TABLE
Instead of | Try
"Why can't you ever listen?" | "I need your eyes for a moment. Then I'll say it once."
"Hurry up!" | "We leave in five minutes. What's left on your list?"
"Stop that right now." | "I need you to stop. Then we can try something else."
"You're being lazy." | "This looks hard. Where do you want to start?"
"I told you a hundred times." | "Let's make a reminder so we both remember."
"What is wrong with you?" | "Something is bothering you. Can you show me?"
"Calm down!" | "I'm here. Let's take a breath together."
"Why did you do that?" | "What were you hoping would happen?"
ENDTABLE

## PB-2 | Phrase Bank: Transitions and Instructions
Short, clear and calm works best for most kids. Say it once, make eye contact and give a moment to respond.

TABLE
Moment | Try Saying
Starting something | "First this, then that."
Getting ready to leave | "Shoes, bag, water. Show me when you have all three."
Ending a favourite activity | "Ten minutes, then we save your place."
Trying something new | "You don't have to do it perfectly. You just have to try."
Before a tricky situation | "Here's what will happen. Here's what I'd like you to do."
When they are stuck | "Do you want help, or do you want me to stay close?"
After a mistake | "Everyone makes mistakes. What can we learn from this one?"
At bedtime | "Today was hard and also you did some good things. Tell me one."
ENDTABLE

## PB-3 | Phrase Bank: Praise That Sticks
Specific praise tells your child exactly what to do again. It sounds like noticing, not grading. Try describing the action, then naming the strength.

TABLE
What You Saw | What You Might Say
They kept trying after a mistake | "You didn't give up. That's persistence."
They noticed someone was sad | "You saw how she felt and checked on her. That's kindness."
They waited their turn | "You waited even though it was hard. That took self-control."
They came up with an idea | "That was a clever idea. I never would have thought of it."
They stayed calm | "You took a breath and settled yourself. That is strength."
They helped without being asked | "You saw what needed doing and did it. That's responsibility."
ENDTABLE

## CLOSING | Keep Going, Hero Guardian
You have come a long way. Maybe you did every mission. Maybe you did three and wrote in the margins. Either way, you showed up for your child, and that matters more than any page in this book.

Some of what you tried worked and some did not. That is how it goes. The goal was never a perfect child or a perfect parent. It was to see your child more clearly, to give their strengths room and to keep your own patience from running dry.

Keep the things that helped. Go back to the Phrase Bank. Reread your Wins Log on a rough day. Do the Five-Second Pause one more time before you speak. Small habits repeated are how families change.

Your child is not a problem to be solved. They are a person to be understood, and you are the one who is learning how.

## SUPPORT | Support and Next Steps
This workbook is a companion, not a substitute for professional guidance. You do not have to do this alone.

**Talk with your child's professionals.** If you have questions about diagnosis, treatment, medication or learning supports, speak with your child's doctor, psychologist or other qualified practitioner. Bring this workbook's notes and the Debrief pages to the conversation.

**Work with the school.** Share what you have learned about your child's strengths and what helps at home with their teacher, school counsellor or special educational needs contact. A short meeting can open up options.

**Find your people.** Many parents find relief in talking to others who understand. Look for local or online parent support groups, community centres and school parent networks.

**Look after yourself.** If you are feeling overwhelmed, low or unsafe, please reach out to your own doctor, a trusted person or local crisis services. Taking care of yourself is part of taking care of your child.

**If anyone is in immediate danger,** contact your local emergency services.
